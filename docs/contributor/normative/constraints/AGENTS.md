# normative/constraints/ — what must stay true

**Frame:** Normative — not-yet truth, in its standing form.

A constraint pins a property that must hold across all present and future states of the project.
It is universal and standing: even when fully satisfied, it remains, governing the space of allowed states rather than describing the current one.
A constraint is the rule that *owns* the question "is the system X?", so new work can't quietly erode the property.

## Reading rule

Never read a constraint as a report that the property currently holds.
It says the property *must* hold; whether it does is checked against ground truth.

## Alignment rule

A violated constraint is at a **distance**, and here the distance is a **violation**: fix the project, or change the rule deliberately — recorded with a decision — never by silently weakening the rule to match what exists.
Urgency comes from the rule's weight, not from the frame.

The descriptive frame must not mirror a constraint: the rule lives here, once, and reports of compliance are not descriptive facts to restate.

For a **pointer** (a rule owned elsewhere — a platform policy, an org standard), you don't control the rule; surface violations and route rule changes to the owner.

## Lifecycle

A constraint **never converts**.
"If satisfied, is it done?" — no.
It is retired only by a deliberate decision to drop the rule.

Per the root contract, fixing a violation or changing a rule is proposed, not performed unprompted.
